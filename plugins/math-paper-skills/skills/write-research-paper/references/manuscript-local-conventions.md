# Resolve manuscript-local conventions

Read this guide when a manuscript's current notation, display conventions, or
explicit owner exception differs from a shared default. Resolve the active
profile through its declared location, including symlinks, and read the actual
paper overlay. Use the designated current profile when archived copies also
exist.

## Establish authority and scope

Preserve the provenance of each convention: current explicit direction,
mandatory venue requirement, local author choice, or an observed example.
Mathematical meaning comes first. Resolve an apparent conflict from the current
conversation and designated records; an imported source file is not a new
author-wide instruction. A newer explicit paper exception can supersede a
default within that paper. Keep that exception within its recorded scope.

Confirm the exact passage, mathematical object, and revision affected by a
local choice. Reuse existing notation and semantic macros where they still
express the intended meaning. When an authorized edit changes the meaning,
update definitions and uses together rather than changing only the visible
symbol. Nearby manuscripts may use the same symbol with a different meaning.

## Apply a local choice without extending it

An owner may permit a particular phrase, a prose statement instead of a display,
or repeated introductory material in separately readable parts. Respect the
recorded choice where it applies; check a concrete ambiguity or contradiction
before proposing a change solely under a general style preference.

Formula line breaks, reference commands, terminology, indexing conventions,
and open-versus-closed ball notation can vary between manuscripts. Read the
paper's definitions and explicit formatting choices before standardizing them.
For example, a paper's choice of `Cref` for internal references does not decide
how to identify an external source theorem. A local rule about breaking a sum
does not prescribe the display layout in another paper.

Use the deliverable's own source and bibliography conventions. A single-file
note or an in-file bibliography does not by itself justify migrating other
manuscripts' tooling or citation systems. Resolve author and source attribution
through [the provenance guide](manuscript-provenance.md).

## Keep individual proof decisions private

Individual proof omissions remain paper-specific decisions under
[the proof-detail policy](owner-proof-decisions.md). Consult only the decisions
needed for the authorized edit and keep their raw provenance in the paper's
private working records. An approved short argument establishes an exposition
choice within its stated conditions; it does not certify the proof or authorize
omitting another mathematical step.

This public guide contains general reconciliation rules and illustrative
conventions. Actual manuscript identities, local notation inventories, and
individual owner decisions belong in the host's private overlay.
